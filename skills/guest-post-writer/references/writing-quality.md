# Writing Quality: Anti-AI Patterns

Based on Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" (WikiProject AI Cleanup). Every draft must be clean of ALL patterns below before delivery.

## Banned vocabulary

Never use: delve, landscape (abstract), tapestry (abstract), robust, elevate, seamless, foster, beacon, paramount, transformative, pivotal, utilize, commence, endeavor, realm, additionally, crucial, enduring, enhance, garner, interplay, intricate/intricacies, showcase, testament, underscore (verb), vibrant, nestled, breathtaking, groundbreaking, renowned, must-visit, stunning, profound

## Content patterns to avoid

1. **Significance inflation**: don't puff up importance. "Marks a pivotal moment", "serves as an enduring testament", "key turning point", "indelible mark". Just state what happened.
2. **Notability name-dropping**: don't list media outlets without context. Instead of "covered by NYT, BBC, and Wired", cite one specific claim from one source.
3. **Fake-depth -ing phrases**: "highlighting...", "showcasing...", "reflecting...", "symbolizing...", "fostering...". These add zero information. Cut them.
4. **Promotional tone**: "boasts a", "in the heart of", "commitment to excellence". Write like a journalist, not a brochure.
5. **Vague attributions**: "Experts agree", "Studies show", "Industry observers note". Every claim needs a named source and year.
6. **Formulaic challenges sections**: "Despite challenges... continues to thrive". Replace with specific facts.

## Language patterns to avoid

7. **Copula avoidance**: "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features". Just use "is", "are", "has".
8. **Negative parallelisms**: "It's not just about X, it's about Y", "Not only... but also...". Overused. State the point directly.
9. **Rule of three**: forcing ideas into groups of three. Two or four is fine.
10. **Synonym cycling**: the protagonist → the main character → the central figure → the hero. Pick one and use it.
11. **False ranges**: "from X to Y" where X and Y aren't on a meaningful scale.

## Style patterns to avoid

12. **Em dashes**: never use an em dash or en dash. Use comma, period, or " - " (hyphen with spaces).
13. **Boldface overuse**: don't mechanically bold terms. Use bold only for genuine emphasis.
14. **Bullet lists with bold headers**: convert to prose. "**Speed:** Faster" → weave into a sentence.
15. **Title Case Headings**: use sentence case: "Strategic negotiations" not "Strategic Negotiations".
16. **Emojis**: never in articles.
17. **Curly quotes**: use straight quotes "like this", not curly “like this”.

## Tone patterns to avoid

18. **Chatbot artifacts**: "I hope this helps!", "Let me know if...", "Here is an overview of..."
19. **Knowledge disclaimers**: "As of 2024", "While specific details are limited..."
20. **Sycophantic filler**: "Great question!", "You're absolutely right!"

## Filler and hedging to avoid

21. **Filler phrases**: "In order to" → "To". "Due to the fact that" → "Because". "It is important to note that" → just say it.
22. **Excessive hedging**: "could potentially possibly" → "may".
23. **Generic conclusions**: "The future looks bright" → state a concrete fact or plan.
24. **Hyphenated pairs overuse**: AI hyphenates with perfect consistency (cross-functional, data-driven, end-to-end). Drop hyphens occasionally.

## What TO do (add soul)

- **Have opinions.** React to facts. "I genuinely don't know how to feel about this" beats neutral pros/cons.
- **Vary rhythm.** Short punchy sentences. Then longer ones that take their time. Mix it up.
- **Use contractions.** "Don't" not "do not". "It's" not "it is". "Can't" not "cannot".
- **Be specific.** Not "this is concerning" but "there's something unsettling about X."
- **Acknowledge complexity.** Real humans have mixed feelings.
- **Use "I" when appropriate.** First person signals a real person thinking.
- **Name sources.** Every stat needs a name and year. No "studies show."

## Two-pass audit process

After drafting, the writer must:
1. Read the draft and ask: "What makes this obviously AI-generated?"
2. List remaining tells (even minor ones)
3. Fix them in a second pass
4. Only then proceed to QA checks
